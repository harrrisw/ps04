It wasn’t the workshop I thought I was doing.
I just finished up my fourth workshop with the wonderful Lab Products Association, and it wasn’t the workshop I thought I was doing.
Months ago I had proposed an agenda and that’s what got published online. A week before the workshop, a survey went out to registered attendees with questions like “What is the ONE thing you need to learn at this workshop to make it worth your investment?”
This isn’t a new question or a new idea. Asking your audience what would make an event a success is always a great idea.
When I looked at the responses that came in, 6 days before the workshop, I realized I had a serious problem. What I wanted to teach, no one wanted to learn. Despite how we marketed it, none of the agenda items were the attendees’ burning questions. What attendees really wanted, I could teach, but it wasn’t what I planned.
So the entire thing went in the trash a week out from the event.
I fired up my speaker second brain that I talked about in my newsletter a couple weeks ago to see what I had in stock. And I had a gap, a sizable gap. 5 days before my workshop, I decided to write down my thoughts about those gaps, and that became last weekend’s newsletter.
Then last night at the opening night reception, I talked to some of the folks who hadn’t filled out the survey, and over ravioli and prosecco, I finalized the agenda. The night before the workshop. Granted, there weren’t a ton of tweaks, but some of the material got more focused on very, very specific needs and use cases.
And today? The workshop went off without a hitch. I got to all the material I wanted, I hit my times exactly, and in side conversations, attendees told me they were getting exactly the value they’d need to judge the event a success.
The big thing I learned from this is that the jagged edge of AI is INCREDIBLY jagged. Some folks had deep skills in one part of AI but never touched others, and one aspect in particular that’s been in market for a couple of years now is still absurdly underutilized – Katie and John talked about that today on the Trust Insights livestream, deep research. In the workshop, I showed a deep research report that half the attendees wanted to take home because the information was immediately usable by their sales teams.
For both speakers and events, the faster we can pivot to meet the needs of our audiences, the happier they’ll be and the more they’ll recommend our events as “can’t miss” value. With things like second brains, you can build to order in hours instead of weeks, and make your audiences deliriously happy.
