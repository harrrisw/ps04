Lexical? Semantic? Both!
Two words that you should be thinking about as you use AI, in particular for larger projects where you have multiple documents, are lexical and semantic. These are the kinds of searches that AI, with the appropriate support tools, can use to dramatically reduce token usage AND generate better results.
Lexical search is what you’d call traditional search, exact match or very close match search. “Portable espresso maker” would find, well, portable espresso makers. It’s precise. It will also miss related concepts that are not word-level matches, like “portable coffee maker”.
Semantic search is topical search, searches where you’re looking at concepts that are related. “Portable espresso maker” would find coffee brewer, instant coffee, crema, a recipe for an Irish coffee, and maybe even Starbucks earnings reports. It finds related topics, so if you had a massive library of documents, it would quickly cull that down to the coffee-related things – but the tradeoff is precision. If you want a document about portable espresso maker, you might be okay with an espresso drink recipe but probably not Starbucks earnings reports.
What if you could have both? You can! This is a classical information retrieval technique (not generative AI) called reciprocal rank fusion, where you create a blend of parallel searches. RRF software runs a lexical search and a semantic search, then derives a blended result from both. A document that scores well in both lexical and semantic search shows up first in the results.
Today’s agentic AI tools, if given the right support technologies, can do both very well. Two that are super useful, completely free, and very powerful are zvec-grep and QMD. zvec-grep does an amazing job, especially with code, at finding related code using reciprocal rank fusion. QMD does an equally amazing job on big documents collections like internal wikis or just folders filled with hundreds or even thousands of documents.
NEITHER uses cloud AI LLMs – but both have connectors that agentic tools like ChatGPT Work, Claude Code, etc. can use, and you will be amazed at how much smarter and faster your agents get. And because neither uses expensive cloud APIs or subscriptions, neither will run up your token bill – in fact, it’ll likely cut it down significantly.
Lexical and semantic are like peanut butter and chocolate. They go together and they make generative AI much more powerful.
