Use AI at the peak of the previous best.
If you want to get the most benefit out of AI without expending the most resources (and cost), here’s a simple rubric for you to remember: use AI at the maximum level of the previous generation.
For example, Anthropic released Opus 5.5 yesterday. Record-breaking performance, faster, better, etc. etc., the usual marketing copy we’ve all heard. But what’s useful from their benchmarks is the generational chart.
On their chart, they have the best of the best from the previous generation, Fable 5.1 (the green line). You can see that Fable 5.1 tops out around a score of 55 (vertical axis).
What does that correspond to in the new model? Opus 5.5 at medium effort. Not high, not xhigh, not max, but medium.
Why? Because as brains increase, effort decreases. Today’s state of the art models are so much better than previous generations that you can use them at lower, faster, cheaper settings than the previous generation at full blast.
Likewise, if you’re used to GPT-5.6 Sol at full blast, you can get away with Opus 5.5 at low setting and still get equivalent results.
You don’t need to operate AI dialed all the way up to 11, not all the time. If you want great performance AND lower costs/environmental impact, choose the model effort settings that correspond to the previous generation’s best efforts. As brains go up, effort goes down, and you’ll get faster, better, AND cheaper results.
