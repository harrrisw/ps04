**Narration:**
The next thing to watch is whether vendors move beyond announcements and publish worked comparisons. We should want named baselines, plus the full cost of setup, checking, and correction [claim:03-c3]. That matters because being good at prompting is not the same as being good at judging output quality [claim:03-c3]. For a proposed comparison, give your current process and the AI-assisted process the same question and documents. Record setup, checking, corrections, and the quality of the final answer. Keep missing sources and unresolved contradictions visible. These are suggested evaluation steps, not results we have measured. A quicker first draft would not establish a saving if it required more checking afterward [claim:03-c3].

**Narration:**
There is also a practical design lesson from recent AI assistant research: retrieval and model quality are not the same thing [claim:03-c4]. If a tool cannot tell you what sources it searched, or what it missed, then it is not yet a strong evidence tool [claim:03-c5]. That is why the best small-business use cases stay human-led: the AI does the prep, and people keep the critical judgment [claim:03-c5] [claim:03-c6].

So the short version is: pilot AI where the task is narrow, source-linked, and reversible. Use it to organize evidence, not to replace verification.
