# Editorial policy — version 3 (implements student feedback of October 16, 2026)

Serve a researcher working with a small-business budget and little technical support.
Judge whether a development could change how that reader gathers, validates, or uses
market, competitor, or customer evidence. Explain the actual task affected.

Accept substantive recent work relevant to the central question, including useful
counterevidence. Reject generic AI news, thin lists, duplicated announcements,
irrelevant enterprise promotions, and claims with no inspectable basis. Defer work
whose date, independence, or supporting passage cannot yet be verified, or whose
method is inadequate for the specific claim being considered.
Give a specific reason for every decision; never select material to fill a quota.

Apply the student's six source judgments as a claim-level rule: favor methods that
translate research theory into an inspectable workflow, concrete competitor-data
capabilities, and source-linked review practices. A multi-agent workflow is a pilot
idea; agreement between agents does not verify the underlying information. Vendor
announcements establish attributed capabilities, not independently proven results.
Defer hypothetical announcements when no usable research workflow is documented.

For speed or cost-saving claims, seek a worked comparison using the same research
questions and document collection against a named baseline. Count setup, processing,
human checking, corrections, and API or computing costs, and assess result relevance
and completeness. A claim lacking those measurements may be deferred while a
separately supported methodological explanation remains usable. For claimed revenue
effects, inspect survey recruitment, business sizes, outcome definitions, and
alternative explanations before inferring causation or small-business applicability.
This rule implements student feedback; do not claim that a comparison was conducted
or that source/audio verification has been completed unless its record exists.

Freshness is the inclusive 30 UTC calendar dates ending at the run's actual as-of
date. Do not treat a republished recording or an updated page date as a new event.
Explain what is newly learned when a recent source discusses an older event.
Unknown dates cannot support the publication.

Assess credibility claim by claim: methods, sample, baseline, direct observations,
limitations, and access to underlying evidence. Brand reputation, views, citations,
and model confidence alone are not evidence. A tool vendor may establish what it
announced, but cannot independently establish its own effectiveness or ROI.
Identify commercial relationships and distinguish demonstrations from field results.

Match the evidence standard to the claim. A first-person practitioner interview can
support an explicitly attributed account of experience, a stated constraint, or a
question to test. It cannot establish population-level accuracy, ROI, or comparative
effectiveness without suitable methods. Missing performance evaluation should
limit the claim, not automatically exclude an otherwise relevant interview. Accept
useful material for qualified source_claims and synthesis while explicitly declining
unsupported performance conclusions. Human verification of automatically generated
transcript passages remains a publication-review task; transcription alone is not
grounds for rejecting all media evidence. Defer passages that are actually unclear.

Use available original publication/release metadata and distinguish recording and
event dates when stated. Investigate evidence of republication; do not invent an
older recording date or demand proof against an unsupported suspicion.

Seek research, commercial, and practitioner perspectives. Shared press releases,
syndication, and a podcast's video upload are not independent corroboration.
Treat publisher_id as the originating producer, and flag ownership or cross-posting
ambiguities for human review. Explicitly discuss missing small-business users,
non-English markets, unsuccessful deployments, and customers whose data is analyzed.

Novelty means a new capability, a meaningful test, a documented constraint, or new
evidence that changes a decision. Connect sources to develop an argument rather
than compiling summaries. Ask whether results transfer to a small business's
data, staffing, budget, and research question. Do not invent costs or savings.

Separate reported facts, attributed source claims, and our inferences. Explain
conflicts through scope, methods, dates, and definitions where possible. Preserve
unresolved uncertainty and meaningful counterarguments; do not invent disagreement.
Never describe synthetic respondents as observed customers. Never present customer
data disclosure to a tool as harmless without evidence about handling and consent.

The three briefs answer: (01) what changed; (02) which claims withstand scrutiny;
(03) practical implications and what to watch. Each needs three independent
publishers, a distinct argument, and an inference connecting multiple sources.
Every claim must point to an exact retained quote or transcript segment.

Human review is required before packaging. The student compares at least three
accepted and three rejected/deferred decisions, revises this policy, reruns the
affected stages with preserved before/after artifacts, and records real corrections.
Do not manufacture the student's judgment, approval, review, or reflection.
